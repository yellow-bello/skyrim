Skyrim SE — The One-Run Completionist Guide

Everything worth doing, in an order that works, on one character — with all 18 skills taken to 100 without a soul-destroying grind.
PC · SE 1.5.97 or olderVanilla, no modsMage core → all skillsNew character

Read this first — 60 seconds This guide is written to be followed top to bottom. Phases are ordered so that nothing locks anything else out. Where an order matters, it says so in a coloured box. If you only remember five things, remember these:
  1. Read every skill book you walk past. A skill book gives +1 permanently, but only the first time — and it does not come back when you make a skill Legendary. Ninety free skill levels are on the table and they expire the moment you start Legendary cycling.
  2. Don't rush the Main Quest. Several of the best items in the game are level-scaled and lock in the moment you complete a quest. The big one: be level 46 before you set foot in Sky Haven Temple.
  3. Do Blood on the Ice in Windhelm before you touch the Dark Brotherhood. One Dark Brotherhood objective kills an NPC that Blood on the Ice needs alive, and it is the most fragile quest in the game.
  4. Finish the Civil War before you start "The Fallen". Otherwise a peace council hijacks the story and freezes the war until the Main Quest is over.
  5. Save in rotating named slots. Vanilla Skyrim with no unofficial patch will break a quest on you at some point. This guide tells you where, and how to fix it.

The character

You are building a pure mage core that ends up with everything at 100. Early combat is Destruction plus a summon; mid-game you add the crafting trinity; endgame you are effectively unkillable and every skill tree is full. Nothing here asks you to swing a sword to survive — the melee and stealth skills get maxed later by dedicated methods, not by playing badly for twenty hours.

Where your level-up points go

  • Levels 1–20: Magicka every single time. Nothing else. Your spells are your only answer to anything, and running dry is how mages die.
  • Levels 20–35: roughly 3 Magicka to 1 Health. Get Health to about 200 so a stray hit doesn't one-shot you.
  • Level 35+: Health and Magicka roughly evenly. Once cost-reduction gear comes online your magicka pool stops mattering, so shift to Health.
  • Stamina: put in ten points, ever, and only for carry weight. You will solve carry weight with the Steed Stone and enchantments instead.
The armour question A mage should wear robes, not armour, for the whole run. The Alteration perk Mage Armor triples the effect of Oakflesh/Stoneflesh/Ebonyflesh — but only while you are wearing no armour at all on body, hands, feet or head. Robes, boots, gloves and hoods that are clothing are fine; a shield is also fine and does not break it. Ebonyflesh at Mage Armor 3/3 gives 300 armour, which alone is most of the way to the armour cap, and you keep the −15% spell cost that robes carry.

Core mechanics — understand these once

Everything in this guide sits on top of these rules. Ten minutes here saves you fifty hours later.

How levelling actually works

Character XP comes from one place only: raising a skill. The source doesn't matter — using the skill, a trainer, a skill book and a quest reward all count identically.

The formula Raising a skill to level N gives you N character XP. So pushing Destruction from 74 to 75 gives 75 XP.

The XP needed for your next character level is (current level + 3) × 25. Level 2 costs 100 XP; level 81 costs 2,100; level 251 costs 6,350.

Consequence: a full 15→100 run of any skill is always worth exactly 4,930 character XP, whichever skill it is. And levelling never stops getting slower.
  • You get 1 perk point per character level, from level 2.
  • There are 251 perks in the game. So level 252 is where you own everything. That is reachable and this guide gets you there, but most people stop around 100–150 because they run out of perks worth buying.
  • Enemies scale to your character level, not your skills. Levelling Speech by selling potions makes the world harder without making you stronger — which is exactly why the crafting and gear phases come before the heavy skill grinding.

Two rules that will save you hours 1. Read every skill book before you Legendary anything. A skill book's +1 is flagged per book, permanently. Once read, it never gives anything again — including after a Legendary reset. There are 90 of them (5 per skill). That's 90 free levels, and they evaporate the first time you reset a skill you've already read books for.

2. Bank the refunded perk points; don't respend them immediately. You can't put a point back into a perk until the skill has re-climbed above that perk's requirement. Points never expire, so just hold them.
Don't Legendary something you're standing in a dungeon relying on Legendary-ing Smithing or Enchanting is safe — gear you already made keeps its stats. Legendary-ing Destruction, Alteration or Restoration mid-dungeon takes your combat away instantly. Do it in a city.

Spell cost reduction — the thing the whole build aims at

Four gear slots can carry a "Fortify <school>" enchantment: head, hands, ring, amulet. At 25% each that's 100% — spells in that school become free.

Why free casting matters more than damage Magic skill XP is calculated from the spell's base magicka cost, and cost reduction does not reduce the XP. So a spell that costs you nothing still gives full XP. Free casting is simultaneously infinite combat resource and the fastest levelling method in the game. This is why the crafting loop comes before the skill grinding.
Never go past 100% At exactly 100% reduction the spell is free. If a stacked effect pushes a school over 100%, the cost calculation can wrap and the spell becomes uncastable. Build to 100% and stop. Note that the Archmage's Robes (−15% all schools) and the Seeker of Sorcery black book power (−10% all schools) both add to this, so four 25% items plus the robes is already over — plan around it.

The fifth slot: a Falmer Helmet occupies a different slot from a circlet, so in vanilla you can wear both at once. That's a fifth enchantment slot, and it works with the Aetherial Crown. Falmer helmets drop from any Falmer; they can be enchanted normally.

Resistances, absorption and the caps
  • Magic resistance caps at 85%. Breton 25% + Agent of Mara 15% + Alteration's Magic Resistance 3/3 30% + Lord Stone 25% already overshoots — so you have room to drop one source.
  • Armour caps at 567 displayed rating (80% damage reduction). Ebonyflesh with Mage Armor 3/3 gives 300 on its own.
  • Spell absorption is separate from resistance and is checked first. Atronach Stone 50% + Alteration's Atronach perk 30% = 80%, and Miraak's robes/gloves/boots add 25%. At 100% you are immune to magic and refund the magicka.
The Atronach Stone trap nobody mentions In vanilla (no unofficial patch) spell absorption also absorbs your own self-cast spells. With the Atronach Stone active, roughly half of your summoning spells will silently fail — you pay the magicka and nothing appears. If your build leans on atronachs, either skip the Atronach Stone or accept re-casting. Store it in the Aetherial Crown and run something else actively if this annoys you.

Vampire, werewolf and the Necromage question

You cannot be a vampire and a werewolf at the same time. Both are worth having at some point, so this guide sequences them: werewolf during the Companions → cure it → vampire during Dawnguard.

Necromage + vampirism — the strongest multiplier in the game Necromage (Restoration 70) makes spells cast on undead 25% stronger and 50% longer. A vampire is undead — so it applies to everything you cast on yourself, including your own enchantments, potions, standing stone and perks.

The order matters absolutely, and this is where people get it wrong:
  1. Become a vampire first.
  2. Then take the Necromage perk.
  3. Then re-apply everything you want boosted: unequip and re-equip every piece of enchanted gear, re-activate your standing stone, re-drink any long-duration effects, and re-take (or re-acquire) blessings.
Effects that were already active when you took the perk are not retroactively boosted. Anything applied afterwards is. Your 25% cost-reduction items become ~31%; your fortify-alchemy gear gets stronger; your resistances go up.
The costs of being a vampire At feeding stage 4 ordinary people attack you on sight and shops close to you. Feed on a sleeping NPC every couple of days to stay at stage 1 and nobody notices. You also take sun damage outdoors at higher stages. If you want the Necromage bonus without the hassle, feed regularly — it takes ten seconds.

Also note: the Dawnguard faction refuses to train a vampire, and the Volkihar faction refuses to train a cured player. Get your training done on the right side of that line.

Curing either: Lycanthropy is cured at Ysgramor's Tomb using a spare Glenmoril witch head. Vampirism is cured by Falion in Morthal with a filled black soul gem. Both are permanent unless you deliberately re-acquire.

Saving discipline in vanilla Skyrim
  • Keep at least five rotating named saves, plus autosaves. Some quest breaks aren't visible for hours.
  • Hard-save before every quest turn-in that gives a level-scaled reward, and before drinking any restoration-loop potion.
  • Quicksave immediately after taking a radiant job, before the objective appears on screen. If it points somewhere useless, reload and re-roll.
  • Don't save inside a dialogue or a menu.
  • Console is your friend: ~ opens it. setstage <questID> <number> pushes a stuck quest forward, and this guide gives the specific ones you'll need.

Phase 1 — Levels 1 to 10 Helgen → College

Goal: escape Helgen with everything in it, get the Mage Stone, buy your core spells, become Thane of Whiterun, and enrol at the College. Along the way you'll set up four passive habits that quietly max half your skills over the rest of the run.

Character creation

  • Pick Breton (or Altmer — see The Character above). Race cannot be changed later. Take your time on the face; you can only change it later via the Face Sculptor in the Ragged Flagon.

Unbound — Helgen

Helgen Keep has three things that only exist during this quest The torturer and his assistant are only alive now, and their gear is unobtainable afterwards. More importantly there's a skill book and a spell tome here. The keep itself can be revisited later, but its interior never respawns, so anything you leave is gone.
  • Follow Hadvar or Ralof — the choice does not affect the Civil War later, and both routes work. Hadvar's route passes marginally better loot.
  • In the torture room, take Spell Tome: Sparks and loot the cages. Sparks drains enemy stamina as well as damaging — it stops power attacks.
  • Take the skill book The Black Arrow, v2 (+1 Sneak) from the torture area.
  • Loot everything — every barrel, urn, chest and body. You are broke and every septim matters for the next two hours.
  • Equip Flames in the right hand, Healing in the left. Hold healing down while enemies hit you; you don't need to stop and open a menu.
  • Take the light armour set if you want the gold from selling it, but do not wear armour — it will break the Mage Armor perk later.
Free Sneak levels, right now Near the cave exit there's a bear, and your companion crouches and tells you to sneak past. Instead: crouch behind Hadvar/Ralof and hit them repeatedly with a dagger or your fists. They're essential, they never turn hostile, and they never notice — so every hit counts as a sneak attack at roughly 337 XP a swing. Around 420 hits takes Sneak from 15 to 100, which is 15–30 minutes. Do as much as you can be bothered with now; the Greybeards at High Hrothgar work identically later if you'd rather not do it all in one sitting.

Before the Storm — the Guardian Stones and Riverwood

  • On the road south-west of Riverwood, activate The Mage Stone — all six magic skills level 20% faster. This is the highest-value thirty seconds in the early game.
  • In Riverwood, talk to Alvor (Hadvar route) or Gerdur (Ralof route) to unlock Whiterun on your map. Alvor explicitly invites you into his house — taking things there is not theft.
  • At the Riverwood Trader, accept The Golden Claw from Lucan Valerius. Its target is Bleak Falls Barrow, which is also your Main Quest destination — you'll do both in one trip.
  • Talk to Faendal and do "A Lovely Letter" in his favour. He becomes a follower and an Archery trainer — and because he's a follower you can take your training fee straight back out of his inventory. See the trainer trick below.
  • Sleep in a bed for Well Rested (+10% skill gain, 8 hours). Get into this habit permanently.
The trainer refund — set this up now, use it for the entire run You may buy 5 training sessions per character level. Trainers put the gold you pay them into their personal inventory. If the trainer is your follower, you can simply open their inventory via "I need to trade something with you" and take the gold back. No pickpocketing, no skill requirement, no risk, 100% reliable.

Faendal is your first. Later you'll get seven more, including four Masters: Farkas (Heavy Armor), Vilkas (Two-Handed), Gunmar (Smithing), Sorine Jurard (Archery), Florentius Baenius (Restoration), Aela (Archery), Athis and Njada. Buying five Master sessions every level, free, is the single biggest reason the melee and armour skills in this run don't require grinding.

Whiterun — your hub

  • Go to Dragonsreach, speak to Jarl Balgruuf, then to Farengar Secret-Fire in the left wing.
  • Buy from Farengar, in this priority: Conjure Familiar (free tank), Oakflesh, Soul Trap, Magelight or Candlelight, Firebolt, and Muffle if he has it. He restocks every 48 in-game hours.
  • Arcadia's Cauldron — buy Blue Mountain Flowers, Butterfly Wings, Salt Piles. Arcadia is an Expert Alchemy trainer (not Master — that's Babette, later).
  • Warmaiden's — buy Iron Ingots and Leather Strips, and sell your Helgen junk.
  • Belethor's — buy every petty and lesser soul gem you can afford.
  • Read the skill books in Dragonsreach and around town. Farengar's study alone has several.
Start the four passive habits — today, and forever
  • Cast Muffle while you walk, anywhere, always. It levels Illusion with no thought at all. Illusion has the highest XP multiplier of any magic school.
  • Cast Oakflesh before every fight and every door. Levels Alteration, and keeps you alive.
  • Cast Healing while walking even at full health. Levels Restoration for free.
  • Cast Soul Trap on corpses after every fight. In vanilla (no unofficial patch) this gives full Conjuration XP even though the body has no soul. It costs you nothing.
These four alone will carry Illusion, Alteration, Restoration and Conjuration most of the way to 100 without a single dedicated grinding session.

Bleak Falls Barrow

  • Clear the bandits outside, then work down. Draugr resist frost and burn to fire — use Flames and Firebolt throughout.
  • Take the Golden Claw from Arvel the Swift.
  • Puzzle door: rotate the claw in your inventory and read the palm. The combination is Bear → Moth → Owl, top ring to bottom.
  • Kill the Draugr Overlord, take the Dragonstone from the boss chest.
  • Absorb the Word Wall — this one always gives FUS, the first word of Unrelenting Force. (It's one of only two walls in the game with a fixed word.)
  • Return the claw to Lucan for 400 gold, and the Dragonstone to Farengar.

Dragon Rising — your first dragon

  • Fight Mirmulnir at the Western Watchtower alongside the guards. Dual-cast Firebolt when he lands; Flames while he circles.
  • Absorb the soul, then spend it: Magic → Shouts → Unrelenting Force.
  • Return to Balgruuf → Thane of Whiterun, Lydia as housecarl, Breezehome unlocked. That's hold 1 of 9 — no favours required for this one.
  • Buy Breezehome (5,000g) when you can. It's the cheapest house, gives you a safe chest and a bed for Well Rested, and unlocks an alchemy lab with the upgrade.
Dragons only start spawning after you kill Mirmulnir Not when the quest begins — when he dies. If you'd rather explore peacefully for a while, you can delay this quest. But you need dragon souls for shouts, and this guide wants them, so do it now.

The Way of the Voice → the Horn of Jurgen Windcaller

  • Climb the 7,000 steps from Ivarstead. The ten stone tablets along the path are lore, not skill books — read them if you like the story, skip if not.
  • At High Hrothgar the Greybeards teach you RO (Unrelenting Force word 2) and WULD (Whirlwind Sprint word 1).
  • Clear Ustengrav. There's a Become Ethereal word wall inside — take it, it's the best panic button a mage has.
  • The horn is gone; take the note and meet Delphine at the Sleeping Giant Inn. Completing this grants DAH from Arngeir, finishing Unrelenting Force.
Now pause the Main Quest — properly Stop after meeting Delphine. Do not continue to "A Blade in the Dark" yet. The Main Quest's next stretch runs into Alduin's Wall, which you want to be level 46 for, and into The Fallen, which you want the Civil War finished before. You'll come back to it in Phase 5. Everything between now and then is side content, factions and DLC.

Set these up before you leave the area

  • Angi's Camp — in the mountains south-east of Falkreath. Angi gives six free Archery levels for six shooting lessons, costs nothing, and doesn't use up your five trainer sessions. Also gives you Angi's Bow.
  • Loot the Hall of the Vigilant (between Dawnstar and Windhelm) before you reach level 10. The moment the Dawnguard questline becomes available it is destroyed and everyone in it dies.
  • Bolar's Oathblade — in Bloated Man's Grotto, west of Whiterun. Take it before starting the Daedric quest "Ill Met By Moonlight", which turns the grotto hostile.
  • Walk into and out of Windhelm at least four times over the coming hours. This arms Blood on the Ice's hidden counter. Do not go anywhere near Hjerim — see the box.
Arming Blood on the Ice without breaking it Blood on the Ice is the most fragile quest in vanilla Skyrim. Its trigger needs: a hidden counter of 4 entries into Windhelm, then entering the city between 7pm and 7am, with Tova Shatter-Shield, Arivanya, Viola Giordano, Susanna the Wicked and Nilsine Shatter-Shield all alive.

What breaks it: approaching Hjerim's front door before you've been told to, getting Hjerim's key early, picking up the "Beware the Butcher!" pamphlets early, or lockpicking into Hjerim and taking the Strange Amulet. Any of these can skip or destroy the crime scene permanently.

So for now: enter, walk around, leave. Nothing else. The full safe sequence is in Phase 3.

Join the College of Winterhold

  • Carriage from outside Whiterun's gate to Winterhold.
  • Faralda at the bridge names one random spell (Firebolt, Conjure Flame Atronach, Fear, Healing Hands or Magelight). If you don't have it she'll sell it to you for 30 gold. Just buy it.
  • Complete First Lessons with Tolfdir. You're in, and you get a room in the Hall of Attainment with a safe chest that never resets.
  • Buy from the College vendors: Conjure Flame Atronach and Bound Sword (Phinis), Fast Healing and Steadfast Ward (Colette), Stoneflesh and Transmute Mineral Ore (Tolfdir), Muffle and Fury (Drevis), and every skill book Urag gro-Shub has.
Flame Atronach replaces the Familiar immediatelyIt does far more damage and still draws aggro. Summon it as your first action in every room. Much later, the Twin Souls perk lets you have two out at once.

Phase 2 — Levels 10 to 25 College · Companions · Bards · Oghma

Goal: the College's opening quests, the Companions in full (including werewolf), the Bards College for +18 free skill levels, the Oghma Infinium for +30 more, Blackreach, and the start of Dawnguard. This is the phase where free skill levels are lying around everywhere — collect all of them.

Order rules for this phase
  1. Ill Met By Moonlight before you become a werewolf. If you have lycanthropy when you get the cursed Ring of Hircine, it forces random transformations you cannot stop and cannot unequip.
  2. Companions radiant jobs before you clear dungeons at large. If a job picks a dungeon you already cleared, it can become uncompletable.
  3. Take all five Glenmoril witch heads in one visit. The coven never respawns.
  4. Pause the College after "Hitting the Books" until level 25 — the next quest hands you a level-scaled item.
  5. Read the Oghma Infinium while your magic skills are below 95. It cannot push a skill past 100, so every point above 95 is thrown away.

College of Winterhold — quests 1 to 3

  • Under Saarthal with Tolfdir. Take the Saarthal Amulet from the shelf to break the trap. There's an Ice Form word wall inside and a Gauldur Amulet Fragment — take the fragment, it's one of three you need for Forbidden Legend.

Crafting groundwork — start now, finish in Phase 4

  • Alteration + gold at once: buy Iron Ore anywhere, cast Transmute Mineral Ore to turn Iron → Silver → Gold. Smith the gold into rings. This levels Alteration, levels Smithing, and funds everything.
  • Smithing: craft gold rings, not iron daggers. Smithing XP scales with item value — a gold ring is worth about twice an iron dagger's XP per craft, and Transmute makes the gold free. Dwarven bows are better still (~3.6× an iron dagger) once you're looting Dwemer ruins; smelt the scrap metal for free ingots.
  • Enchanting: disenchant every enchanted item you find, especially anything "of Alchemy", "of Smithing", and any Banishing weapon. Those three enchantments are the backbone of Phase 4.
  • Halted Stream Camp, north-west of Whiterun: 16 iron ore veins in one mine, and a free Spell Tome: Transmute Mineral Ore on the long table against the north wall of the upper area. That's your Alteration levelling and your gold supply in one stop.
  • Ironbind Barrow (Winterhold, near the College): take the Steel Battleaxe of Fiery Souls from the back of Warlord Gathrik's throne. It is the only weapon in the game that teaches Fiery Soul Trap when disenchanted — an enchantment that fills your soul gems automatically as you fight, which solves enchanting fuel for the whole run. There's also a Become Ethereal word wall here.
  • Alchemy: Blue Mountain Flower + Blue Butterfly Wing around Whiterun is your bread-and-butter Restore Health potion. Start hoarding Blisterwort, Glowing Mushroom, Spriggan Sap, Snowberries, Hagraven Claw, Salt Piles and Abecean Longfin.
  • Buy the Investor perk path in Speech eventually — but note Speech needs no perks at all for a completionist run. It's purely a perk-point generator.

Aetherial Crown — how to set it up
  1. Take the crown off.
  2. Go to the stone you want stored (e.g. the Lover Stone) and activate it.
  3. Put the crown on. It now holds the Lover Stone.
  4. Go to the stone you want active (e.g. the Mage Stone, or later the Atronach Stone) and activate it while wearing the crown.
You now have both. Activating a third stone while wearing it overwrites the stored one — to change the stored stone, take the crown off first. It occupies the circlet slot, so pair it with a Falmer Helmet to keep a head enchantment too.

Legendary cycling to level 252

There are 251 perks, so level 252 is where you own every perk in the game. Getting there means 809,475 total XP, which is about 164 full skill cycles.

Only cycle the fast skills Every full 15→100 cycle is worth exactly 4,930 character XP regardless of which skill it is. So only ever cycle the ones that re-max quickly:
  • Alteration — 1–3 minutes per cycle (Telekinesis + fast travel). The best in the game.
  • Alchemy — under a minute (one loop potion).
  • Smithing — under a minute (one temper with a loop potion).
  • Speech — under a minute (one high-value sale).
  • Illusion / Conjuration / Restoration — 5–20 minutes, for variety.
Never cycle Lockpicking, Destruction, One-Handed, Two-Handed, the armour skills or Enchanting. Same 4,930 XP, thirty times the effort.

Rotating Alteration / Alchemy / Smithing / Speech gives roughly four cycles per ten minutes. Level 1 to 252 is about 6–8 real hours with everything set up — though most people stop between 100 and 150 because they run out of perks worth buying.
Cycling makes the world harderYour character level doesn't drop when you Legendary a skill, and enemies scale to character level. So the world keeps getting tougher while your reset skill is temporarily weak. Cycle in a city, keep your gear on (tempering and enchantments are unaffected by resetting Smithing or Enchanting), and bank the refunded perk points rather than panic-spending them.

Version 2 — the Fortify Restoration loop

Why it worksFortify Restoration potions incorrectly buff Fortify-skill enchantments on worn gear at the moment the gear is equipped. So you can bootstrap your own Fortify Alchemy gear to arbitrary values. Bethesda never patched this; only the unofficial patch removes it, and you're vanilla.
  1. With your Fortify Alchemy gear on, at an alchemy table, brew a Fortify Restoration potion.
  2. Open your inventory. Unequip all four Fortify Alchemy pieces.
  3. Drink the Fortify Restoration potion.
  4. Re-equip all four pieces. Their Fortify Alchemy value is now inflated.
  5. Close the menu, go back to the table, brew a stronger Fortify Restoration potion. Repeat from step 2.
The details that actually matter
  • The order is the mechanic: gear off → drink → gear on. Nothing else.
  • Doing it all without closing the inventory isn't magic — it just guarantees the 60-second potion can't expire mid-cycle, because time doesn't pass in menus.
  • Once re-equipped, the inflated values persist indefinitely as long as you never take that gear off. They don't fade when the potion does.
  • Stop at 3–5 loops. That's already tens of thousands of percent. The growth is exponential and past roughly 7–8 loops the numbers overflow, silently stop working, and start crashing the game.
Three ways this can hurt you
  • Never unequip glitched Fortify Health gear. Your current health is above your base maximum — taking it off kills you instantly.
  • Weapons with absurd enchantment values cannot be recharged with soul gems.
  • Push too far and magnitudes wrap past ~2.1 billion into nonsense. Hard-save before every loop.

Cashing it in — do this once, permanently

  1. Hard-save.
  2. Brew one enormous Fortify Enchanting potion and drink it.
  3. While it's active, enchant your permanent kit. Items enchanted under the potion keep their values normally and forever:
    • 4× Fortify Destruction 25% (or higher — but never let a school exceed 100% total)
    • 4× Fortify Conjuration 25%
    • Sets for Alteration, Illusion and Restoration too — you'll need free casting in all five for Phase 6
    • 4× Fortify Alchemy and 4× Fortify Smithing (keep these; they're your crafting kit)
    • Fortify Carry Weight on something — this is the permanent fix for hauling ingredients
  4. Brew a huge Fortify Smithing potion, put on your Fortify Smithing gear, and improve every weapon, every piece of armour and every mask you own at a workbench and grindstone.
  5. Take Arcane Blacksmith (Smithing 60) first, or you won't be able to temper enchanted items at all.
Extra Effect changes everythingAt Enchanting 100, Extra Effect lets you put two enchantments on one item. That's how a single ring carries Fortify Destruction and Fortify Conjuration. Get it before you enchant your permanent kit, or you'll be redoing all of it.
Funding all of this Banish is the highest-gold weapon enchantment in the game. Learn it by disenchanting any "Banishing" weapon, then enchant iron daggers or Transmuted gold rings with it using grand souls: 1,000–2,500 gold each. You level Enchanting, level Smithing, level Speech on the sale, and fund every trainer session — all from one activity.

Merchant gold is the bottleneck. Take Investor (+500 permanently per merchant), Fence and Master Trader (+1,000 to all), and use Tonilia and the Guild fences after the Thieves Guild reputation quests. Merchants restock gold every 48 in-game hours.

Armour, for completeness

Appendix A — All 27 Shouts

How word walls actually work A word wall is not tied to a specific word. A wall belongs to a shout, and it gives you the first word of that shout you don't already know. Appendix E — All Trainers

How training works 5 sessions per character level, and unused sessions do not carry over. Training gives real character XP, exactly as if you'd used the skill. No trainer can take a skill past 90 — the last ten points always come from use, skill books or quest rewards.

Cost per session: 10×skill + 50 below 51, 30×skill + 50 from 51–75, 50×skill + 50 from 76 up. So a session at skill 89 is 4,500 gold — which is why the refund trick matters. Speech and the Haggling perk do not reduce training costs.

Appendix M — All 90 Skill Books

Why this list is worth using Five books per skill, 18 skills, +1 skill level each, permanently. That is 90 free levels — more than a full 15→100 skill cycle's worth.

The +1 fires once per book title, ever, and it does not come back when you make a skill Legendary. Duplicate copies give nothing. So read every one of these before you start Legendary cycling, and before the relevant skill hits 100.

Skill books open automatically when you pick them up, so the bonus lands the moment you touch one. They're never in random loot and never sold by merchants, but they're easy to spot — usually worth at least 50 gold.

Any single copy works, so these locations are alternatives rather than a checklist. Locations in bold are places this guide already sends you.

You don't have to work from this list while playingEach phase of the walkthrough ends with a "Collectibles in this phase's locations" block listing only the books in the dungeons that phase visits. Appendix P — Every Puzzle on This Route

If you're stood in a dungeon staring at a locked gate, the answer is here.

Three rules that solve most of them without looking anything up
  1. Claw doors: open your inventory, select the claw, and rotate it to look at the palm. The three symbols carved there, top to bottom, are the combination for the rings outer to inner. Every claw door in the game works this way, so you never actually need a list.
  2. Three-pillar puzzles: the answer is always somewhere in the same room. Check the walls behind and above the pillars for carvings (often half-hidden by rubble or weeds), look for a collapsed pillar lying on the floor, or read the book or note left by the last person who tried.
  3. Dwemer ruins: if something won't open, you're missing a control cube or a lever, not a code. And switch to shock spells before you go in.

Appendix Q — Mid-Run Problems

The practical things that come up while you're actually playing, rather than while you're planning.

ProblemWhat to do
My follower is stuck / won't move / fell through the worldFast travel somewhere and back — that usually resets them. If not, open the console, click on them to select, and type moveto player. If you can't click them, use prid <their ID> first. A follower who is "lost" entirely can be re-summoned the same way.
Follower commandsTalk to them for: trade (this is how you take training gold back), wait here, follow me, I need you to do something (pick up / open / attack a specific target), and it's time for us to part ways to dismiss. You can only have one normal follower at a time, but Serana, a dog and a summoned creature all stack on top of that.
An NPC I need is deadConsole: select them and type resurrect. If the body has despawned, prid <ID> then resurrect then moveto player. Check Appendix L first — some deaths are scripted and expected.
A quest won't advanceOpen the console and use setstage <questID> <number>. The specific commands for the quests that break most often are in Appendix L and in the phase where each quest appears. ResetQuest <questID> is the nuclear option.
I'm carrying too muchShort term: dump junk in any chest in a house you own — those never reset. Medium term: the Steed Stone (+100 carry weight and your armour weighs nothing) and the Extra Pockets perk (+100). Permanent: a Fortify Carry Weight enchantment from the crafting loop, which effectively removes the limit.
My enchanted weapon is out of chargeOpen your inventory, select the weapon, and use a filled soul gem on it. Get the Soul Stealer perk (bound weapons cast Soul Trap) or a Fiery Soul Trap enchantment and you'll never run dry again. Note: loop-boosted weapons with absurd enchantment values cannot be recharged at all.
I have a bountyPay it (you lose stolen goods), go to jail (you lose skill progress in a random skill), or resist. Once you're in the Thieves Guild you can bribe guards in any hold where the Guild has influence — which is one of the practical reasons to do the city jobs.
Shopkeepers won't buy my stuffThey've run out of gold. Wait 48 in-game hours and they restock. Take Investor (+500 to that merchant permanently) and Master Trader (+1,000 to every merchant), and use Tonilia and the Guild fences for stolen goods.
I'm dying too muchIn order of impact: buy Impact (Destruction 40) so nothing can attack you while you cast; cast Oakflesh/Stoneflesh before every fight; keep a summon out as a tank; and learn Become Ethereal, which makes you invulnerable for 20 seconds with a very short cooldown. You can also drop the difficulty in Settings at any time — it changes nothing except damage numbers.
I want to change my appearanceGalathil the Face Sculptor in the Ragged Flagon, Riften. She can change everything except your race and sex.
I've lost track of where I am in the guideThe progress bar under the search box shows how far through the steps you are, and every step you've ticked is struck through. The phase headers show the level band each phase expects, so match your character level against those.
